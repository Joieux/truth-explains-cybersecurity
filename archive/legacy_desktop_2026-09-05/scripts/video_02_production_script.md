## ORIGINAL SCRIPT — RHYTHM PROBLEMS MARKED

```
Auntie's back! Last time, we talked about the foundation. Today, let's fix
that first layer: those messy passwords.

Think of a password like a key to your house. [Visual: Focus Scene - The
Rusty Key] Right now, most people are out here using that same one key for
the front door, the car, the office, and the safe.

That's not confidence. That's a horror movie setup!

And here's the tea — the way they get in isn't what you see in the movies.
Hackers aren't guessing your password by hand.

They buy lists. Huge lists from sites that got breached. Then they take your
password from one site and try it everywhere else.

That's called credential stuffing. Same trick, new site, all day long.

[PROBLEM 1] "That's how I break it down, Aunt Truth style." — This line
doesn't connect. It's an EP01 holdover. Cut it.

I know that sounds heavy, but here's how we're gonna flip the script.
[PROBLEM 2] "So, what are we gonna do?" — Redundant. The next line answers
it immediately. The question doesn't add anything.
Two things, and they're easier than you think.

First, you need a password manager. [Visual: Focus Scene - The Master
Keyring] Think of it like a locked keychain that works for you. You create
one strong master password — the only one you have to remember — and then
the manager makes unique, complicated passwords for every single site you use.

So even if one site gets breached, your other accounts don't fall like
dominoes.

[PROBLEM 3] "Now, while the manager does the heavy lifting..." — "Now" pivot.
Third time in two episodes. Swap it.
Second, stop making passwords that look like Summer2025! or Password123!.
If I can guess it, a script definitely can.

[PROBLEM 4] "Now, I know what you're thinking..." — Another "Now" pivot.
Swap it.
Good question. Stay sharp!

Password managers aren't magic, but they are built for this. They protect
your vault, they're constantly monitored, and they're updated way more often
than that one password you've had since 2012.

The biggest risk isn't the manager — it's you reusing the same weak keys
everywhere.

Here is your simple starter plan:
1. Pick a password manager you trust.
2. Turn on auto-fill (make it easy on yourself!).
3. Start with your email, then your bank, then your socials.

One at a time. No panic. Just progress. You're taking control now, baby.
That's growth.

[Action: Coffee Sip]
Alright, family. That's enough for today.

If this helped you, stay with me. We're building understanding, not rushing
perfection.

[PROBLEM 5] "Now, passwords are a great start, but next time I'm showing you
the deadbolt that keeps them safe even if the keys get stolen." — Another
"Now" pivot AND a run-on. Split it.

I'll see you next time. We've got this.
```

---

Auntie's back.

Last time we laid the foundation.

Today we fix that first layer — the one most people overlook: those messy passwords.

Think of a password like a key to your house.

Right now, most people are using that same one key for the front door, the car, the office, and the safe.

That's not confidence.

That's a horror movie setup.

Here's the tea — the way they get in isn't what you see in the movies.

Hackers aren't guessing your password by hand.

They buy lists. Huge lists from sites that got breached.

Then they take your password from one site and try it everywhere else.

It's called credential stuffing.

Same trick, new site, all day long.

It's not personal. It's just volume.

I know that sounds heavy.

Here's the good news: two things.

That's all you need. And they're easier than you think.

First: a password manager.

Think of it like a locked keychain that works for you.

You create one strong master password — the only one you have to remember — and the manager makes unique, complicated passwords for every single site you use.

So even if one site gets breached, your other accounts don't fall like dominoes.

Second: stop making passwords that look like Summer2025! or Password123!.

If I can guess it, a script definitely can.

Those aren't passwords — they're an open door.

I already know your follow-up question:

"Auntie, what if the password manager gets hacked?"

Good question. That's exactly the right thing to be thinking about.

Password managers aren't magic.

But they are built for this.

Your vault is constantly monitored, patched, and updated — way more often than that one password you've had since 2012.

The biggest risk isn't the manager.

It's you reusing the same weak keys everywhere.

Here's your starter plan — three steps, that's it.

Pick a password manager you trust.

Turn on auto-fill so you're not fighting it.

Then start small: your email first, then your bank, then your socials.

One at a time. No panic. Just progress.

You're taking control. That's growth.

Alright, family. That's enough for today.

If this helped, stay with me.

We're building understanding, not rushing perfection.

Passwords are your first layer.

Next time, I'm adding the deadbolt — what keeps your accounts safe even if those passwords get out.

I'll see you then.

We've got this.
