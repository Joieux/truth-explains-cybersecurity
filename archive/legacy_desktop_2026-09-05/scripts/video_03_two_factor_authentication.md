# Aunt Truth: Cybersecurity for Real
## Episode 03: Two-Factor Authentication, Without the Confusion

[Visual: Auntie's Desk - Opening Frame]
So we’ve got our keys in order with that password manager, right? Now we’re adding the deadbolt. Let's talk **Two-Factor Authentication**, or 2FA. 

Let's make it make sense.

[Visual: Focus Scene - The Heavy Vault & Deadbolt]
Think of it like this: your password is the first lock on your door. 2FA is that second lock—the deadbolt that stays locked even if somebody manages to swipe your first key. 

Because even if a bad guy gets your password, they still can't get in without that second proof that it's actually you.

That second proof usually comes in three flavors:
1. **Something you know** (like a PIN).
2. **Something you have** (like your phone).
3. **Something you are** (like your fingerprint/face ID).

Those are the 'what,' but we need to talk about the 'how.'

[Visual: Pull back to Auntie's Desk]
[Action: Coffee Sip]
But baby, here is the real talk: not all 2FA is built the same. 

A lot of y'all are using those text message codes. Now, that’s better than nothing, but we gotta be aware of **SIM swapping**. That’s when a hacker tricks your phone company into moving your number to *their* phone. Now *they* get your codes, and you’re locked out of the house.

That’s how I break it down, Aunt Truth style.

But Auntie wouldn't leave you with just the problem. Here is the upgrade: **Authenticator apps**. 

They live right on your device and they aren't tied to your phone number. No phone company middleman, just you and your security. 

And if you really wanna go pro? Get yourself a hardware key or use **Passkeys**. It’s like having a physical key that only you can hold.

I know that's a lot of tools, so let's keep it simple. Here’s your Auntie-approved upgrade path:

1. **Email first**: Your email is the master key to everything else. Lock it down with an authenticator app.
2. **Bank account**: Secure your coins, baby.
3. **Socials**: Don't let nobody hijack your voice.

The goal isn't to be a perfect tech genius by tomorrow. The goal is layers. 

Not panic. Not perfection. Just layers.
