`[AT DESK: LIB-01 — Warm opening, turns to camera]`

Hey, family! I am so glad you showed up today!

Alright — let's have some real talk:
cybersecurity sounds like something for people
who own server racks and have three monitors.

It's not.

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-02 — Auntie Sip — unhurried, she's about to settle something]`

And here's what I want you to sit with.

You've been doing cybersecurity your whole life.

You just didn't have a name for it.

---

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 01 — Cartoon woman at apartment door, checking peephole]`

Think about it — you lock your door every night,
and you check who's there before you open it.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 02 — Man on sidewalk refusing to hand keys to stranger]`

And you would never —
not in a million years —
hand your house keys to somebody you just met on the street.

That's it. That's cybersecurity.

---

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 03 — Digital life flatlay: phone, laptop, emails, photos, bank statement, DMs]`

Because those doors are everywhere now.

Your phone, your laptop, your email —
your bank, your photos, your whole identity —
all of that is a door now.

Everything you've worked for.
Everything you count on.
Sitting on a device you sometimes use
*half-asleep on the couch.*

`[AT DESK: LIB-07 — Pointing direct at camera]`

And because it's worth something to you —
it's worth something to somebody else too.

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-05 — Slow knowing head shake]`

Now. The person trying to get through those doors?

They're NOT what the movies told you.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 04 — Unremarkable scammer in messy apartment, eating chips, no hoodie]`

Not a genius.
Not somebody in a dark hoodie with dramatic music playing.
Just — a regular person.

Might be in sweats right now.
Eating some chips.
Running the same copy-paste message
to a *thousand* different people.
At the exact same time.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 05 — Email template, cursor clicking Send, name cycling automatically]`

Same template.
Different name.
Hit send. Move on.
*All day long.*

`[AT DESK: LIB-07 — Pointing direct]`

They're not hunting you specifically.
They're fishing.

Casting a big net over wide water.
Just waiting for whoever bites.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 06 — Triptych: tired woman at 1am / rushing man in crowd / distracted person with 40 tabs]`

And who bites?

Usually the person who's tired.
The one who's rushing.
The one who's got forty tabs open
and didn't really read the email before clicking the link.

We've all been there.
And that is *exactly* what they're counting on.

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-03 — Leaning in, soft and direct]`

And that's why I'm telling you this.

Not to scare you.

But to *steady* you.

Because once you actually see how simple this game is —
you stop being the person they can play.

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-04 — Counting on fingers — deliberate]`

So let me give you the map.

Every cybersecurity threat on this earth —
every single one —
comes down to three things.

They call it the CIA Triad.
And no — not *that* CIA.

Confidentiality. Integrity. Availability.

Let me walk you through each one.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 07A — Gold padlock snapping shut over floating icons]`

The first one is *Confidentiality* —

which is just a fancy word for keeping private things private.

Your passwords. Your bank info.
Your personal messages.
The photos that are yours and nobody else's *business.*

If someone who shouldn't see it can see it —
that's a breach of confidentiality.

`[AT DESK: LIB-06 — Confident, grounded — this one matters]`

Simple as that.
Your private life stays yours.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 07B — Same padlock — now with a crack, shadowy eye behind it]`

The second one is *Integrity* —

keeping your information accurate, meaning *true.*

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 08 — Gradebook on screen, shadowy hand changing A to D]`

Picture somebody quietly changing your grade,
or altering the amount on a payment,
or tweaking something in your medical record.

You never knew it happened.
But it did.

The data's still sitting there —
it just *isn't true anymore.*

That's an integrity attack — and the scary part is, you might never find out.

`[AT DESK: LIB-05 — Head shake — this one's important]`

And the third one is *Availability* —

being able to *get to* your own stuff when you need it.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 09 — Woman at laptop, screen goes red, ransomware lock]`

Say a ransomware attack locks your files.
Or a system crashes and your account goes dark.

Your data still exists —
you just can't *reach it.*

And safe data you can't access?
That's not actually safe for *you.*

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-03 — Leaning in for the wrap]`

That's the CIA Triad.

Every breach, every scam, every attack —
is going after one of those three things.

That's the whole game.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 10 — Four protection rings stacking: password, manager, 2FA, awareness]`

And here's the good news —
you protect yourself with *layers.*

Not panic.
Not expertise.
Just habits.

Use a strong password.
Keep it in a password manager —
so you're not reusing the same one everywhere.
Because that is how one breach turns into *ten.*

Use two-factor authentication —
because that's a second lock on the door.

And the habit that matters more than all of them combined:

Slow. Down.

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-08 — Firm stop gesture, palm up]`

And that last one —
I want to sit with that for a second.

Because *this* is the thing I really need you
to take out of this video.

If something feels *urgent* — stop.
If someone is *rushing* you — stop.
If it feels like pressure — stop.

That IS the attack.

`[CUT TO: B-ROLL 11 — Cartoon hand hovering over suspicious link, pausing, lowering phone face-down]`

That urgency you feel?
It was *engineered.*
On purpose.

Every scam works because it makes you feel
like you don't have time to think.

That one second where you pause —
where you *don't* click —
that is your entire defense.

The pause is the power.

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-06 — Warm, confident, settled]`

And look —

You don't have to become a hacker to protect yourself.

You just need habits that make you harder to fool.

And you've already got what it takes.
That's why you *showed up.*

---

`[AT DESK: LIB-02 — Auntie Sip — unhurried, like the end of a good conversation]`

Alright, family.
That's enough for today.

If this landed —
stay with me.

We're building this together, understanding first, tools second.

`[AT DESK: LIB-09 — Warm wave, quiet smile]`

Next time we're talking passwords — what makes one actually strong, how to stop forgetting them, and why your dog's name with a one at the end —
*I'm looking at you* —
is absolutely not it. I'll see you then. We've got this.

## WHAT CHANGED FROM v2 — AND WHY

| Old line | Problem | New version |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| "I see you out here tryna level up, but I heard some of y'all are a little stressed" | Weak opener. Puts the viewer in a passive, stressed position right away. | Opens with a flip — "You've been doing this your whole life." Viewer is capable from word one. |
| "Pull up a seat. We're gonna take it slow. No gatekeeping." | Sounds like a disclaimer. Signals low energy. | Replaced by Auntie Sip as a beat. The *action* communicates warmth. |
| "That's it. That's the whole energy. That's how I break it down, Aunt Truth style." | Three wrap-up phrases back to back. The last one is an orphaned line. | One clean landing: "Same instinct. Different door." |
| "But wait—to understand the defense, you gotta understand what we're actually defending." | Seam. You hear the writing. | Removed. Scene transitions naturally into what we're defending. |
| "It's your whole routine, baby. And since your whole life is on there..." | "Baby" lands soft. The pivot to threat is weak. | Replaced with "half-asleep on the couch" — visceral, specific, true. |
| CIA Triad: "First... Second... Third..." read like a PowerPoint | No stakes, no scenario. Just definitions. | Each pillar now gets a concrete scene — grades changed, payment altered, ransomware. You feel what the breach *costs.* |
| "Now here is an Aunt Truth rule that will save your life" | "Now" pivot again. "Save your life" is hyperbole that undercuts the calm authority. | Replaced with "Here's the one thing I need you to carry out of this video." Specific, personal, no drama needed. |
| "You've got the spark." | Vague. Doesn't mean anything. | Replaced with "you've already got what it takes — that's why you're here." Ties back to the viewer's choice to show up. |
